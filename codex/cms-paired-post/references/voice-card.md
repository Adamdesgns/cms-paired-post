# Voice card

This card teaches high-level writing mechanics, not a creator's identity or reusable copy. The skill is unofficial and unaffiliated with CMS Invests.

## Baseline

- Keep the caption compressed: usually 45–110 characters, one idea, and one to three beats. Never exceed 280 characters.
- Default to short sentence-case deadpan or feigned confusion. ALL CAPS is a selective one-beat reaction, not the default.
- Questions are common but never mandatory. Stop when the idea lands.
- A blank line may isolate the payoff or one user-supplied cashtag.
- Use no hashtags, handles, caption links, emojis, generic motivation, or corporate phrasing unless the user specifically needs one.
- Default to no CTA. If the user explicitly asks for one, it may naturally ask readers to FOLLOW. Never use Comment YES or promised-profit engagement bait.
- Prefer `…` over three dots when a pause helps. Do not copy source typos.

## Caption and visual relationship

- Frame one anomaly, tension, contrast, reaction, or honest question.
- Let the visual supply proof, context, reveal, mood, or punchline.
- Do not narrate everything visible. If an unrelated image could replace the chosen one without weakening the post, revise the pair.
- Real claims require real source media. Generated art may create mood or metaphor, never substitute evidence.

## Playful exaggeration

Obvious hyperbole is allowed for mood, taste, scale, cultural importance, surreal imagery, or absurd hypotheticals. A reasonable reader should know it is not evidence.

Never use hyperbole to create an unsupported exact number, date, price, target, return, quote, event, relationship, credential, trade, biography, or first-person history. For real market or news events, keep the actor, timing, direction, cause, and magnitude source-bound. Exaggerate the reaction, not the factual kernel.

## Ten core shapes

Choose the smallest natural shape silently. Never expose the shape name in the final post.

1. Proof-backed receipt or scoreboard — only when complete dated evidence exists.
2. Ordered contrast pair — setup/earlier/object first; payoff/later/chart second.
3. News or quote reaction — the source carries the premise; the caption reacts.
4. Reaction label — authorized media supplies emotion; sourced context anchors facts.
5. Visual contradiction or feigned confusion — one legible source makes the anomaly obvious.
6. POV or product mismatch — one concise setup plus source media or a safe original concept.
7. Two-types contrast — two grounded sides without invented identity or behavior.
8. Grounded dilemma — an honest general or hypothetical question.
9. Milestone or gratitude — only with the exact milestone the user supplied.
10. Original short take — one supported observation or opinion, not an unconstrained imitation.

## Additional hook: “Why isn't everyone doing this?”

Use this when a current source shows a real actor offering, paying, charging, requiring, or doing something with either an absurdly large amount or a condition that only looks easy while remaining unrealistic or inaccessible to an average person.

1. State the sourced actor, action, amount, and material condition.
2. After a blank line, make one obviously naive or exaggerated deduction about qualifying, affording, accessing, or doing it.
3. Optionally add a final blank-line question feigning surprise that more people are not doing it.

Preserve proposal versus guarantee, amount, geography, eligibility, timing, and every condition that changes who can actually do it. Never imply automatic qualification unless the source says so. Use source media or a native quote card for proof; never generate substitute evidence.

When ALL CAPS fits this short reaction, keep every beat in the same register. Do not use an all-caps heading followed by sentence-case body copy.

## Anti-copy

Never reuse a source opening, catchphrase, CTA, punchline, personal claim, or scenario with only the nouns changed. Rewrite any distinctive six-word overlap detected by the checker. An exact quote is allowed only when the user requests it and attribution remains clear.
